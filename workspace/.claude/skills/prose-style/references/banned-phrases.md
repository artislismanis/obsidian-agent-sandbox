# Banned phrases

Quick lookup. Search this file before keeping a suspicious phrase.

## Adverbs

Cut all `-ly` words and these specific offenders:

- really, just, literally, genuinely, honestly, simply, actually
- deeply, truly, fundamentally, inherently, inevitably
- interestingly, importantly, crucially, notably, particularly
- essentially, basically, generally, virtually
- absolutely, completely, entirely, totally
- very, quite, rather, fairly, somewhat

**Test:** delete the word. If the sentence still works, leave it deleted.

## Hedges

These weaken claims. Commit or admit ignorance.

- might, may, could, would (when speculating)
- could potentially, may possibly (redundant)
- seems to, appears to, tends to
- kind of, sort of
- I think, I believe, I would suggest, I would recommend
- perhaps, arguably, presumably
- in some ways, in a sense

If you do not know, say so: "Unclear whether X."
If you do know, drop the hedge: "X is true" beats "X seems to be true".

Exception: "I recommend" is fine when you are the named recommender in a
review or decision document. The skill bans hedging *as* recommending,
not stating your recommendation.

## Throat-clearing openers

State the content. Skip the runway.

- "Here's the thing:"
- "Here's what [X]" / "Here's why [X]" / "Here's this [X]"
- "The uncomfortable truth is"
- "It turns out"
- "The real [X] is"
- "Let me be clear"
- "The truth is,"
- "I'm going to be honest"
- "Can we talk about"
- "Look,"
- "So,"

Any "here's what/this/that" construction is throat-clearing. Cut.

## Emphasis crutches

These add no meaning.

- "Full stop." / "Period."
- "Let that sink in."
- "Make no mistake."
- "This matters because" (state the reason directly)
- "Mark my words"
- "Trust me"

## Meta-commentary

The document should move, not announce itself.

- "Hint:"
- "Plot twist:" / "Spoiler:"
- "You already know this, but"
- "But that's another post/PR/ticket"
- "The rest of this doc explains..."
- "Let me walk you through..."
- "In this section, we'll..."
- "As we'll see..."
- "I want to explore..."

## Vague declaratives

Sentences that announce importance without naming the thing. Kill.

- "The reasons are structural"
- "The implications are significant"
- "This is the deepest problem"
- "The stakes are high"
- "The consequences are real"
- "There are many considerations"
- "Several factors are at play"

If you cannot name the specific implication or reason, cut the sentence.

## Lazy extremes

Cut when they do vague work:

- every, always, never
- everyone, everybody, nobody
- all, none
- constantly, perpetually

Use specifics. "Three of our five customers" beats "every customer".

## Transitional language

Prose describes the current state.

- now, currently, still
- used to, previously, formerly, originally
- no longer, as of
- going forward, moving forward

## Filler phrases

Cut these. They add no information.

- at its core
- in today's [X]
- it's worth noting
- at the end of the day
- when it comes to
- in a world where
- the reality is
- in the process of
- for the purpose of
- with respect to
- in the context of
- as mentioned above
- as previously stated
- please note that

## When to add to this list

A phrase belongs here when:

- It adds no information, AND
- It pattern-matches as AI writing or corporate fluff, AND
- You have seen Claude Code (or anyone) produce it in this project's prose.

Add new phrases under the closest existing category. If no category fits,
add a new section, then update the index at the top of this file.
